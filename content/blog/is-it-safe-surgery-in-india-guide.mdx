If you're considering traveling abroad for treatment, safety is the first and most important question. Here's a practical, honest breakdown of what makes surgery in India safe — and what to check before committing.

## The Short Answer

Surgery in India is safe **when you choose an accredited hospital and a reliable care coordinator**. India has a large number of internationally accredited hospitals with outcomes comparable to Western centers, but standards vary widely across the country's thousands of hospitals — due diligence matters.

## What Makes a Hospital Safe for International Patients

**1. Accreditation.** Look for **JCI (Joint Commission International)** and/or **NABH (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals)** certification. These confirm the hospital meets structured, audited standards for infection control, clinical protocols, and patient safety — don't hesitate to ask the hospital directly for a copy of their current certificate.

**2. Surgeon credentials.** Verify the surgeon's qualifications, years of experience with your specific procedure, and any international training or fellowships. India's National Medical Commission (NMC) maintains a public registry of licensed practitioners for verification.

**3. International patient infrastructure.** Reputable hospitals have a dedicated international patient department — interpreter support, coordinated discharge planning, and a single point of contact throughout your stay.

**4. Transparent, itemized pricing.** A hospital or facilitator that won't give you a clear written cost breakdown before travel is a red flag.

## Red Flags to Watch For

- Reluctance to share accreditation certificates or surgeon credentials
- Vague, verbal-only cost estimates
- Pressure to commit or pay large deposits before you've reviewed your treatment plan
- No dedicated international patient support or interpreter access
- Overwhelmingly positive-only online reviews with no specifics (a sign of manufactured testimonials)

## How to Vet a Medical Tourism Facilitator

A facilitator (like a medical tourism company) coordinates between you and the hospital — but the facilitator itself should also be vetted:
- Do they work with multiple accredited hospitals, or push you toward just one (a sign of commission-driven bias)?
- Do they provide written, itemized estimates before you travel?
- Can they show real patient outcomes or verifiable reviews?
- Do they offer support beyond booking — visa help, ground coordination, follow-up care?

## What Safe Treatment Looks Like, Step by Step

1. Medical reports reviewed and matched to an accredited hospital and experienced surgeon
2. Written cost estimate provided before travel commitment
3. Visa and travel logistics coordinated in advance
4. On-ground support from arrival through discharge, including interpreter access
5. Documented follow-up care plan after you return home

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is medical tourism in India safe?**
Yes, when you choose accredited hospitals (JCI/NABH) and a reliable facilitator with transparent processes and on-ground support from arrival to discharge.

**How do I verify a hospital's accreditation?**
Ask the hospital directly for their current JCI or NABH certificate, or check the accrediting body's public registry.

**What if something goes wrong during treatment?**
Ask any hospital or facilitator upfront how complications are handled — including who coordinates extended care and communicates with your family, before you travel.
