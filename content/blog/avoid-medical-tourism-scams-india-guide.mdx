As medical tourism to India has grown, so has the number of intermediaries with little accountability. Protecting yourself starts with knowing what red flags to watch for. Pair this with our shorter checklist on [how to avoid medical tourism scams](/blog/avoid-medical-tourism-scams-india) and the [facilitator selection guide](/blog/how-to-choose-medical-tourism-facilitator-india).

## Red Flag: Pressure to Pay a Large Deposit Before Any Written Plan Exists

Legitimate facilitators provide a treatment plan and cost estimate first — payment discussions come after, not before, you've seen real numbers. [Request a written quote](/get-free-quote) with no obligation.

## Red Flag: No Named Hospital or Doctor Until After You've Paid Something

If a facilitator won't tell you which specific hospital and surgeon will treat you until money has changed hands, that's a serious warning sign. Medical Tours India names partner hospitals upfront on its own site, independently verifiable — start with the [hospital network](/hospital-network).

## Red Flag: No Verifiable Patient Reviews or Stories

Fabricated testimonials are common in this space. Look for reviews that include specifics — country, procedure, hospital — not generic five-star praise with no detail. Compare [patient reviews](/reviews) and [success stories](/patient-stories).

## Red Flag: Unwillingness to Offer a Free Second Opinion

A confident, legitimate facilitator wants you to be sure about your treatment plan before committing to travel — not rushed into a decision. [Free second opinions](/free-second-opinion) exist for that reason.

## What to Do Instead

- Research the facilitator's actual hospital partnerships independently
- Ask for a written quote before any payment
- Request to speak directly with a past patient if possible

A facilitator with nothing to hide will make this easy, not difficult. Accreditation checks still matter at the hospital level — see [how to choose a hospital in India for surgery](/blog/how-to-choose-hospital-india-surgery) and [is surgery in India safe?](/blog/is-it-safe-surgery-in-india-guide).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the most common medical tourism scam in India?**
Pressure to pay before a written plan exists, unnamed hospitals until after a deposit, and generic unverifiable testimonials. Treat any of those as a reason to walk away.

**How can I verify a facilitator is real?**
Confirm named hospital partnerships independently, insist on an itemized written quote, check reviews that name country and procedure, and test their second-opinion process.

**Should I pay a large deposit to a personal account?**
No. Keep communication and payments on official channels tied to a named company and hospital pathway.

**What does Medical Tours India do differently?**
Named hospitals on the site, written packages before commitment, and a no-obligation [second opinion](/free-second-opinion). Apply the same tests to anyone you consider.
