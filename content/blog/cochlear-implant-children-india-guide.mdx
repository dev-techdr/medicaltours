## Who Is a Candidate for a Cochlear Implant?

A child may be a candidate for cochlear implant surgery if they have:

- Severe-to-profound bilateral **sensorineural hearing loss (SNHL)**
- Little or no benefit from hearing aids after a 3–6 month trial
- No medical contraindications to general anaesthesia
- A family committed to post-operative speech therapy and auditory training

> **Important:** For children with **meningitis-related hearing loss**, early evaluation is critical. Cochlear ossification (bone filling the cochlea) can occur within weeks of infection, making late surgery technically difficult or impossible. If your child recently had meningitis and hearing loss, [contact us](/contact-us#enquiry-form) immediately.

## What Is the Best Age for a Cochlear Implant?

This is the question Medical Tours India hears most often from international parents.

| Age at Implantation | Expected Outcome |
|---|---|
| Under 12 months | Optimal — best language development outcomes |
| 12–24 months | Excellent — the plasticity window is still wide open |
| 2–5 years | Good with intensive AVT; some language delay, with catch-up possible |
| 5–12 years | Variable — depends on duration of deafness and AVT access |
| Adults (post-lingual) | Very good — speech recognition improves significantly |

> **If your child is 18 months or younger, do not delay a surgical evaluation.** Every month matters for language development.

### Day 1–2: Arrival and Pre-Operative Workup

On arrival in India, your child undergoes:

- High-resolution **CT scan of the temporal bones** (cochlear anatomy and ossification)
- **MRI of the internal acoustic meatus** (cochlear nerve integrity)
- Comprehensive audiological evaluation
- Paediatric medical clearance for anaesthesia
- A meeting with the ENT surgeon and audiologist

Your Medical Tours India coordinator is with you for translation, transport, and the appointments.

### Day 3–4: Surgery

Cochlear implant surgery typically takes **2–3 hours under general anaesthesia**. The surgeon:

1. Makes a small incision behind the ear
2. Creates a pocket under the skin for the internal receiver-stimulator
3. Drills a small opening into the mastoid and cochlea
4. Threads the electrode array into the cochlea
5. Confirms electrode function with intraoperative **NRT (Neural Response Telemetry)**

Your child is usually back on the ward within 3–4 hours and can eat and move around the same day.

### Day 4–8: Post-Operative Recovery

Children typically stay **4–5 nights in total**. Discharge includes:

- Wound care instructions
- Antibiotic and pain medication
- Activity restrictions for 2 weeks
- A confirmed date for the switch-on appointment

### Day 10–12: Switch-On / Activation

This is the appointment families travel for.

The audiologist activates the external processor and programmes the **initial MAP** (electrode settings). Many children react to sound for the first time. Your child will need **2–3 mapping sessions** during the first two weeks in India. Medical Tours India schedules these before you leave so the processor is set for the journey home.

## What Happens After You Return Home?

A cochlear implant needs ongoing audiology follow-up and **intensive auditory verbal therapy (AVT)** at home. Medical Tours India connects you with:

- A **teleconsultation schedule** with the Indian audiologist for remote mapping adjustments
- A **referral letter** for a local audiologist or ENT in your home country
- Home-based auditory stimulation guidance
- Other cochlear implant families who have already made the trip

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What if something goes wrong after we return home?**
Serious complications from cochlear implant surgery are uncommon at top Indian centres. Device failures are covered by the manufacturer's global warranty. Your Indian surgeon remains reachable for teleconsultation, and Medical Tours India stays your point of contact after you return.

**My child is afraid of hospitals. Will they manage?**
Paediatric teams at partner hospitals are used to anxious children. Play-based preparation and parent presence during anaesthesia induction are standard at the facilities we recommend. Many international families say the child-friendly wards were a relief.

**Is the device available in my country for repairs and upgrades?**
Yes. Cochlear, Advanced Bionics, and MED-EL have global service networks. Spare parts, processor upgrades, and warranty service are available through authorised dealers in most African and Middle Eastern countries. We give you the local service contact before discharge.

**My child has been told they have ANSD (Auditory Neuropathy). Can they still get a cochlear implant?**
Possibly. ANSD needs a specialist assessment. Some children with auditory neuropathy are strong cochlear implant candidates; others may not benefit. Partner surgeons with specific ANSD experience review these cases. Share your child's CAEP and ABR reports and we will advise.
